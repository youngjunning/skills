# Technical-book author introduction

Use this reference for an author introduction in a technical-book proposal, sample chapter, cover or flap, catalog page, publisher submission, or comparable editorial artifact. The goal is a credible explanation of why this author can write this book, not a personal advertisement.

For a publisher-facing package or release artifact, also apply [publishing-requirements.md](publishing-requirements.md). Publisher-specified length, person, placement, photo, contact, disclosure, and production rules override this reference's defaults.

## Establish the editorial slot

Before drafting, identify:

- where the introduction will appear and how much space it has;
- whether it is for publisher evaluation, reader-facing publication, publicity, or an internal review;
- whether it needs a short, standard, or extended version;
- the publisher's word or character limit, required grammatical person, portrait and credit specifications, mandatory fields, prohibited disclosures, and file format;
- which name, pen name, organization, links, and current title are authorized for that audience.

Do not reuse one length mechanically across a proposal, cover, website, and social profile. A proposal may explain book-relevant experience; a cover or catalog entry normally needs a tighter version.

## Build from facts in relevance order

Prefer this information hierarchy, omitting any item that does not help the reader judge the book:

1. author name and pen name, when both matter;
2. current professional role and organization, when disclosure is authorized;
3. years of relevant experience, degree or discipline, and established writing credentials;
4. the role, project, framework, or system most directly related to the book;
5. the technical areas and engineering questions that connect the author to the book;
6. GitHub, personal site, or technical blog, when the publication format benefits from them.

Lead with verifiable identity and book relevance. Do not lead with personality, aspirations, slogans, follower counts, or a list of every technology the author has used.

Use specific relationships between the author and the work. Distinguish accurately among `作者`, `主要作者`, `共同作者`, `架构师`, `参与设计`, and `贡献者`; do not turn participation into sole ownership.

## Fact and disclosure gate

Treat every credential as a factual claim.

- Preserve the user's explicit wording when it is clear and authorized.
- Reconcile conflicting degree, employer, title, date, and experience claims before publication. If a broad but accurate category resolves a terminology mismatch, use it only when it does not conceal a material distinction.
- Do not independently upgrade `工程师` to `高级工程师`, `架构师` to `首席架构师`, or a project role to an industry title.
- For internal products, roles, metrics, customer names, organizational units, or unreleased projects, obtain explicit disclosure authorization. When authorization is narrow, retain the role while removing unnecessary product identifiers and operational details.
- Research publisher conventions separately from personal credentials. Never infer a person's employment, education, awards, or authorship from a weak or unrelated source.
- In the editorial handoff, state which claims came directly from the author and which were independently verified when that distinction matters.

## Professional but restrained voice

Use third person unless the publication explicitly calls for first person. A standard introduction usually works as one or two compact paragraphs:

- first paragraph: identity, experience, writing credential, and the most book-relevant role;
- second paragraph: technical scope, concrete engineering practice, and the problem the author consistently works on.

Prefer concrete nouns and verbs such as `负责`, `担任`, `设计`, `开发`, `维护`, `实践`, and `关注`. Use technical terms only when they clarify the author's connection to the book.

Remove or substantiate promotional language such as:

- `行业领军`, `顶级专家`, `权威`, `大师`, `重磅`, `卓越`, `极具影响力`;
- vague claims such as `深耕多年`, `经验丰富`, `成果丰硕`, or `广受好评` when a concrete fact can replace them;
- self-awarded significance, unsupported rankings, inflated business impact, and adjectives that merely repeat the credential.

A strong introduction can contain several credentials without reading like a title stack. Connect them through the book's problem domain, and stop once the reader can understand the author's qualification and perspective.

## Technical and AI-book fit

For an AI or software-engineering book, emphasize engineering responsibility rather than a generic interest in AI. Useful connections include:

- systems, frameworks, runtimes, infrastructure, products, or open-source work the author actually built or maintained;
- architecture boundaries, reliability, evaluation, security, performance, delivery, or maintainability problems addressed in practice;
- the relationship between the author's experience and the reader promise of the book.

Do not present ordinary API use as AI research, a demo as a production system, or an internal role as public industry authority. Keep `研究`, `研发`, `工程实践`, and `产品经验` distinct.

## Layout and artifact gate

When the introduction is rendered into PDF, DOCX, HTML, or print artwork:

- keep the heading, biography, and contact links together when practical;
- prevent a single contact line, sentence, or link row from becoming an orphan on the next page;
- shorten repetition or tune section-specific spacing before shrinking the document's global type size;
- preserve readable line length, paragraph rhythm, and sufficient whitespace;
- render and inspect the final page at normal reading scale for missing glyphs, fallback-font inconsistencies, clipping, overlap, and awkward page breaks;
- extract the final text and confirm every required credential and link is present.

Do not claim the author introduction is finished merely because the source text is correct; the publication artifact must also be intact.

## Final check

Before delivery, confirm:

- every credential is relevant, authorized, and no stronger than its evidence;
- the introduction explains the author's connection to this specific book;
- no internal detail exceeds the approved disclosure boundary;
- the tone is factual and confident without self-congratulation;
- no copied publisher biography or template phrase remains;
- the final rendered artifact keeps the complete author block readable and together.
